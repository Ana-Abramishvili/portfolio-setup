Post Summary

Aleyda Solís shares insights from her analysis of AI search traffic patterns across 10 international markets, focusing on ecommerce, finance, and travel sectors using Similarweb data.

She challenges the simplified narrative that AI search always favors big global brands and shows that the reality is much more fragmented.

Key findings:

AI search traffic distribution varies significantly by vertical (industry) and country
In many cases, AI search sends traffic to local platforms and infrastructure, not just global brands
Growth in AI search traffic is real, but it comes with high volatility and churn
Finance content tends to be more trust-driven, but patterns are often misinterpreted
Travel is split: discovery is fragmented, but booking behavior is more centralized
Strategic implications:

She outlines several key takeaways for AI search strategy:

AI search should not be treated as a single optimization channel
Local market analysis is essential before scaling internationally
SEO and AI visibility strategies must include local entity optimization + structured data, not just translation
AI search performance should be tracked dynamically, not as static rankings
Brands should adapt strategies based on market structure instead of forcing one global approach
Why this matters

This post highlights that AI search behavior is highly regional and industry-specific, meaning global SEO strategies no longer work uniformly.

It reinforces the importance of local SEO, entity optimization, and market-specific content strategies in AI-driven search ecosystems.

Date: 5 days ago
Source: LinkedIn

Post Summary

Aleyda Solís shares findings from a large-scale technical analysis of over 7 billion web server log files, conducted by Chris Long and Botify, focusing on OpenAI’s crawling behavior.

The research analyzes activity from multiple OpenAI bots and how they interact with the web.

Key findings include:

ChatGPT usage signals suggest potential changes in user behavior patterns
OpenAI may rely partially on cached web resources rather than always live crawling
OpenAI’s web crawling activity has tripled since August 2025
The OAI-SearchBot increased 3.5x in the same period
OpenAI is now spending slightly more resources on search/crawling than training
Despite this growth, Google still dominates web crawling volume by a large margin
Why this matters

This post highlights the rapid expansion of AI crawler infrastructure, especially from OpenAI.

It shows that AI search systems are becoming more dependent on real-time web crawling and indexing behavior, which directly impacts SEO visibility, content retrieval, and AI-generated answers.
