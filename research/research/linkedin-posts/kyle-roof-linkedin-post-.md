Post Summary

Kyle Roof explains how structured SEO content is built and delivered inside his system (POP White Glove). The main idea is that not all content has the same role in SEO, and performance comes from how pages are structured into a content system.

He describes a content silo model, where:

Top Level Content = main page targeting the primary keyword (service, product, or location page)
Supporting Content = articles that strengthen and push authority to the main page

Instead of just “writing content”, they design a system where every page has a clear role in ranking.

He also explains different delivery formats depending on the client:

Content briefs / outlines for internal writers
Optimization briefs for existing pages
AI-generated drafts with SEO structure
Fully human-written and edited content

The goal is to match the content format to the team’s workflow, not force one method.

Post Summary

Kyle Roof talks about why most SEO agencies stop growing around 15–20 clients. According to him, the problem is not sales, but operations.

When agencies reach that point, systems start breaking:

Client work quality drops
Quality control becomes harder
Time shifts from growth to fixing problems

The main issue is lack of structure, not lack of clients.

He explains that simply hiring more people doesn’t solve it because it increases complexity. Instead, the solution is to standardize 80% of the agency workflow, so only a small part stays custom.

This means building repeatable systems for:

SEO audits
Content planning
Delivery processes

With this structure, agencies can scale beyond 50+ clients without losing quality.

Why this matters

This post shows that scaling SEO agencies is mainly an operations + systems problem, not just a marketing or sales problem.

It connects to modern SEO delivery because AI tools and templates make standardization easier, which directly impacts how agencies scale today.
